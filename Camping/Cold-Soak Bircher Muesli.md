# Cold-Soak Bircher Muesli

The original Swiss Bircher muesli is oats soaked overnight in milk and folded with grated apple, which makes it one of the few classic breakfasts that gets *easier* on the trail rather than harder. You are already soaking it overnight at home, so the backcountry version is not a compromise: it is the same technique with a jar in your pack standing in for the fridge. Everything goes in dry at home, water goes in at camp the night before, and breakfast is waiting for you before you have found your headlamp. No stove, no fuel, no pot to scrub in the cold. Dried apple rehydrates in the same soak that softens the oats, so the dish keeps the ingredient that actually makes it Bircher instead of quietly becoming plain overnight oats.

Build it on a scoop of [[Toasted Muesli]] and the whole thing is two minutes of work at the kitchen counter.

### 🛒 Ingredients
- [[Toasted Muesli]] - ¾ C / 80 g
- Whole milk powder - 3 TB / 30 g
- Dried apple, chopped small - ¼ C / 15 g
- Brown sugar or maple sugar - 2 tsp / 10 g
- Cinnamon - ¼ tsp / 1 g
- Salt - pinch
- *optional* Citric acid or powdered lemonade - ⅛ tsp / 1 g
- Water - 1 C / 250 ml

### 🥣 Steps
1. AT HOME: combine everything except the water in a wide-mouth screw-top jar or a heavy freezer bag. Label it. It keeps for a month in the pack.
2. IN CAMP, EVENING: add the water, seal, and shake hard for 20 seconds to break up the milk powder before it clumps.
3. Stash it upright with the rest of your food overnight in a bear hang or canister, never in the tent.
4. MORNING: stir, taste, and eat cold straight from the jar. If it has set thicker than you like, splash in a little more water.

> [!tip] Use whole milk powder, not skim
> Skim powder saves almost no weight and costs you about 40 calories per serving plus all of the mouthfeel. Full-fat powder is what turns the soak creamy instead of watery. It is doing the job cream does in the original recipe.

> [!warning] Below freezing, it will not soak
> Oats need liquid water. On a hard frost night the jar sets solid and you get a muesli popsicle. Put the sealed jar inside a dry bag at the foot of your sleeping bag instead, where body heat keeps it liquid.

> [!note] Pack weight
> `weight-per-serving` is the dry weight you actually carry, 135 g. Water is found in camp and is listed separately under `water-needed`, so this breakfast costs you no fuel and no cookware at all.
