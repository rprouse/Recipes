# Garlic & Cracked Pepper Beef Jerky

Inside round jerky with a garlic-forward marinade, plenty of coarse black pepper, and a gentle chili heat that builds rather than burns.

Almost everything that separates good jerky from leathery disappointment happens before the dehydrator is switched on: how cold the meat is when you slice it, how ruthlessly you trim the fat, and how dry the surface is when it goes on the racks. Get those three right and the drying step mostly looks after itself.

On the trail it is the easiest food in the pack: no stove, no water, no cleanup, and a 50 g handful carries about 30 g of protein, which is the thing most backpacking meals are short on. Trim hard and cure it and it will ride at room temperature for weeks. Unlike [[Dehydrating Ground Beef]], this is finished food rather than a component, so nothing needs to rehydrate at camp.

### 🛒 Ingredients
- 1 kg inside round (top round), fat and silverskin trimmed
- 120 ml soy sauce
- 60 ml Worcestershire sauce
- 60 ml water
- 2 tablespoons brown sugar
- 6 garlic cloves, finely grated
- 2 teaspoons garlic powder
- 2 tablespoons coarsely cracked black pepper, plus more for the racks
- 1 teaspoon onion powder
- 1 teaspoon smoked paprika
- 1½ teaspoons red pepper flakes
- ½ teaspoon cayenne pepper
- ½ teaspoon Prague Powder #1 curing salt (optional)

### 🥣 Steps
1. FIRM UP THE MEAT: Spread the beef on a tray and put it in the freezer until the edges are firm but the centre still gives, roughly 45 to 60 minutes. This is the single biggest factor in getting even slices.
2. TRIM AND SLICE: Trim off every trace of fat and silverskin. Fat does not dry, and it is what turns a stored batch rancid. Slice 4 to 5 mm thick: with the grain for classic chewy jerky, across the grain if you want it to break apart more easily. Consistent thickness matters more than the exact number, so that everything finishes at the same time.
3. MIX THE MARINADE: Whisk together the soy sauce, Worcestershire, water, brown sugar, grated garlic, garlic powder, cracked pepper, onion powder, smoked paprika, red pepper flakes, cayenne, and the curing salt if using. Stir until the sugar dissolves.
4. MARINATE: Put the slices in a zip bag or container, pour the marinade over, and work it around so every piece is coated. Refrigerate 8 to 24 hours, turning the bag once or twice. Twelve hours is the sweet spot.
5. DRAIN AND DRY: Drain the meat and pat each slice properly dry with paper towel, because surface moisture slows everything down. Lay the pieces on dehydrator trays or wire racks without overlapping. Grind a little extra black pepper over the top if you want more bite.
6. DEHYDRATE: Dry at 70°C / 160°F and start checking at four hours. It is done when a cooled piece bends and cracks but does not snap in half, and shows white fibres at the bend.
7. PASTEURIZE (if you skipped the cure): Finish the dried strips in a 135°C / 275°F oven for 10 minutes to bring them to a safe internal temperature. Skip this if your dehydrator genuinely holds 70°C, or if you used the Prague Powder #1.
8. COOL AND CONDITION: Cool completely on the racks, then seal in a jar for 24 hours, shaking occasionally. This evens out the moisture between pieces. If you see condensation on the glass, dry it another hour.

> [!tip] Split the round along its seam first
> Inside round is a big muscle with a seam running through it. Cut along that seam before you do anything else and you get two cleaner, more uniform blocks to slice from, rather than fighting an awkward shape at the board.

> [!warning] Cure it if it is going in a pack
> Prague Powder #1 is optional for jerky eaten at home out of the fridge, but it is what keeps the batch shelf-stable at room temperature, and it adds that faint cured tang. A pack sits in the sun and rides warm all day, which is exactly the condition the cure exists for. If you skip it, do the 10-minute oven finish in step 7, keep the batch in the fridge, and treat it as a day-trip snack rather than multi-day food.

> [!note] Heat level
> 1½ teaspoons of flakes plus ½ teaspoon of cayenne lands at a warm background tingle that accumulates over a few pieces. Double both if you want it to actually bite.

> [!tip] Punchier garlic
> Swap 1 teaspoon of the garlic powder for granulated garlic, sprinkled on after the marinade has drained. It sticks to the surface and stays punchy through drying instead of dissolving into the marinade.

> [!note] Yield and storage
> A kilo of raw round gives roughly 350 to 400 g of finished jerky, so one batch is about a week of trail snacking for one person. With curing salt and a vacuum seal, a month at room temperature is fine. Without it, keep it in the fridge and eat it within two weeks, which will not be a problem. Bag it in daily portions before you leave, because opening one big bag every day at a rest stop is how moisture and grit get in.

> [!note] Numbers are estimates
> This is an original recipe with no published figures, so `weight-per-serving`, `calories-per-serving` and `water-needed` are worked back from the ingredients: about 375 g finished from the 1 kg batch, portioned at the same 50 g used by [[Spanish Trail Mix]]. Trimmed top round plus the sugar that stays behind after draining works out near 3.5 kcal/g, so roughly 175 kcal and about 30 g of protein per portion. Water is `0` because nothing rehydrates, though jerky is salty enough that you will drink more than usual after a handful.
