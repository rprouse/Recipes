# Pad Prik King (Red Curry Chicken and Green Beans)

Adapted from Nuit Regular's "Kiin," this salty-sweet dry curry uses store-bought red curry paste and makrut lime leaves to season sliced chicken thighs and long beans. Curry pastes vary widely in salt and heat, so start with less and add more to taste. The lime leaves are classic, but the dish is still delicious without them.

### 🛒 Ingredients
- 1 teaspoon cornstarch
- ¼ teaspoon fine sea salt
- 1 pound boneless, skinless chicken thighs, cut into ½-by-2-inch strips
- ¼ cup canola or vegetable oil
- 4 teaspoons minced garlic
- 3 to 4 tablespoons red curry paste (see Tip 1)
- 8 ounces long beans or green beans, cut into 1-inch segments (about 2 cups)
- 2 teaspoons oyster sauce (preferably Thai)
- 1 ½ teaspoons sugar
- 12 makrut lime leaves (see Tip 2), thinly sliced crosswise
- Cooked jasmine rice, for serving

### 🥣 Steps
1. Marinate the chicken: In a medium bowl, stir together 2 tablespoons water with the cornstarch and salt. Add the chicken and stir vigorously to combine.
2. Add the oil to a wok or large nonstick skillet and heat over medium. Add garlic and stir until the garlic is fragrant, about 30 seconds. Add the curry paste and mash it into the oil until fragrant, darkened and starting to crumble, about 3 minutes.
3. Add the chicken mixture and cook, stirring frequently, until the chicken is no longer pink, about 4 minutes.
4. Stir in the long beans, oyster sauce and ¼ cup water and cook, stirring occasionally, for another 3 minutes, until chicken is cooked through. Add the sugar and lime leaves and cook for 1 more minute. Serve over rice, or with rice on the side if serving with additional items.

> [!tip] Tip
> **Tip 1, curry paste:** Pastes range widely in intensity. If yours is thick and concentrated and you don't want much heat, use 3 tablespoons; if it's looser or you like it spicy, increase to taste. Start with less, since you can always add more.
>
> **Tip 2, lime leaves:** Fresh leaves give the brightest flavor, but thawed frozen ones work well too (look in Thai or other Asian markets). Dried leaves can be used whole without slicing; use more, as their flavor is muted.
