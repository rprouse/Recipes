# Quick & Easy Refrigerator Pickles

Refrigerator pickles are quick and easy to make — no canning supplies, sterilized jars, or special equipment required. The recipe comes from Joanne Biltekoff, an old friend of Jenn Segal's mother, and once you've had them no deli or store-bought pickle quite measures up. Slice Kirby cucumbers into spears, cover them with brine, tuck them into the fridge, and they're ready to eat the next day. Perfect with burgers, sandwiches, or straight from the jar.

### 🛒 Ingredients
- 1¼ cups distilled white vinegar (5% acidity)
- 3 tablespoons kosher salt
- 2 tablespoons sugar
- 2 cups cold water
- 1¾ to 2 pounds Kirby cucumbers (about 6), cut into halves or spears
- 2 tablespoons coriander seeds
- 6 large garlic cloves, peeled and halved
- 1 teaspoon mustard seeds
- ¼ teaspoon crushed red pepper flakes
- 16 dill sprigs

### 🥣 Steps
1. **Make the brine.** Combine the vinegar, salt, and sugar in a small non-reactive saucepan (stainless steel, glass, ceramic, or Teflon) over high heat. Whisk until the salt and sugar are completely dissolved. Transfer the liquid to a bowl and whisk in the cold water. Refrigerate the brine until ready to use.
2. **Fill the jars.** Stuff the cucumbers into two clean 1-quart (1 L) jars. Add the coriander seeds, garlic cloves, mustard seeds, red pepper flakes, and dill sprigs to the jars, dividing evenly.
3. **Add the brine and chill.** Pour the chilled brine into the jars. If necessary, add a bit of cold water until the brine covers the cucumbers. Cover and refrigerate about 24 hours, then serve. The pickles will keep in the refrigerator for up to one month.

> [!tip] Tip
> Start with Kirby or pickling cucumbers — short, squat, sometimes warty. Don't substitute another kind or you'll end up with soggy pickles. Use a non-reactive pot for the brine; aluminum, copper, or cast iron will react with the vinegar and give the pickles a metallic taste. When the jar runs dry, you can reuse the brine for another batch or two rather than starting from scratch.
