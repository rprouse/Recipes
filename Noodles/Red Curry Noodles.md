# Red Curry Noodles

Lively, piquant stir-fried udon built from the pantry. Store-bought Thai red curry paste does the heavy lifting as a punchy seasoning, bringing savoriness, sharp spice and a complex aroma in one spoonful. Pastes vary a lot in heat and salt, so add more or less to taste; green or yellow curry paste also works. Spinach keeps it fresh, but bok choy, Chinese broccoli, kale or chard swap in easily.

### 🛒 Ingredients
- 28-ounces udon noodles (preferably shelf-stable vacuum sealed or frozen, unthawed), see Tip
- 1 tablespoon neutral-tasting oil, such as avocado
- 1 red or yellow onion, halved and thinly sliced (about 1½ cups)
- 2 tablespoons Thai red curry paste, or more to taste
- 1 tablespoon soy sauce
- 5 ounces spinach (about 5 cups)
- 2 limes, one halved and the other quartered
- 1 cup roughly chopped cilantro, divided
- 4 scallions, trimmed, white and green parts thinly sliced (about 1¼ cups), divided
- ½ cup (2-ounces) roasted peanuts, roughly chopped

### 🥣 Steps
1. Bring a large pot of salted water to the boil. Add the udon noodles and cook according to package instructions, until the noodles separate. Use tongs or chopsticks to gently loosen up the noodles. Drain, rinse well with cold water, and drain again.
2. Heat a wok or large skillet on medium-high for 1 to 2 minutes, until very hot. Drizzle with oil and add the onion, stirring until slightly softened and starting to turn golden, 2 to 3 minutes. Add the noodles (if they have been sitting around a while and are sticking together, rinse with water to loosen them, draining off excess water), 2 tablespoons red curry paste and soy sauce and toss. Taste and check if you need more curry paste, adding another tablespoon if necessary. Add as much spinach as will fit into the pan and toss constantly, piling in more spinach as it cooks down. Keep tossing until all the spinach is wilted, 2 to 3 minutes.
3. Juice 1 lime over the noodles, add half of the cilantro and half of the scallions and toss well. Taste and season with salt.
4. To serve, divide the noodles among 4 bowls, scatter with peanuts and the remaining scallions and cilantro, with a wedge of lime on the side.

> [!tip] Tip
> The udon noodles can be replaced with any dried noodles; you’ll need about 10 ounces.
